# Shipping through release trains

Train mechanics moved out of the global `AGENTS.md` (woksin/pi-setup#158). The ship bar, the merge exception, "never leave a green PR unmerged" and the no-auto-merge rule stay in `AGENTS.md`; this skill holds the train and orchestrator detail. "The exceptions above" below refers to the exceptions in the first bullet.

- **Release trains only: one candidate per repository, even for a single item.** A repository is its main checkout plus all its worktrees. All unshipped work (`pi-train concurrency` reports it) ships in the repository's one open train: one candidate branch, one assembled build and Tier 1, one glue review, one PR, one release. Worktrees keep their own branches, verify only their own change (incremental build of the touched projects, affected specs) and deliver with `pi-train fold` from the worktree (lanes deliver to their repo orchestrator instead). Item branches get no PR: CI and the `run-integration` label go only on the train PR; an item's feedback is its local gates plus its delta review. Never open a PR outside the train; if the candidate hook refuses, fold. The only exceptions are an accepted outsider patch/minor issue after private security review (internal bot captures are not outsiders), a named major release awaiting {{GITHUB_USER}}, or an urgent **security** fix approved in writing by the coordinator, named in the command (`PI_CANDIDATE_EXCEPTION=major|urgent-fix gh pr create …`; `urgent-fix` means a security fix) and in the report.
- **Repo orchestrator (multi-lane setups).** When two or more lanes or workers are active in one repository, it has one **repo orchestrator**, between the global coordinator (L0) and those lanes; the coordination rules in force (`lanes status`, or `PI_ORCH_ID` with pi-orch) name it. Brief it from `~/.pi/agent/templates/repo-orchestrator-brief.md`. It owns:
  - **Work split:** which lane takes which issue, file ownership between lanes, and the merge and dependency order of branches.
  - **Builds and tests:** one gate per candidate code state, a schedule for the heavy phases, and an attribution of every failure to the delivery that caused it.
  - **PRs and releases:** only the repo orchestrator pushes, opens PRs and merges in that repository, as trains (several issues, one release). It labels them, writes the release note, merges and watches Publish.
  - **Decisions:** it makes and records repository-local design and scope decisions, escalating only what crosses repositories or needs a product decision.
  - **Inbox SLA:** it acknowledges every delivery within one wake cycle, replying with the candidate SHA or a reasoned rejection.

  With `lanes`, the second lane in a repository gets a repo orchestrator (`add --orchestrator ID` promotes an existing lane; `--no-orchestrator` opts out), and lane reports to L0 route through it unless `send --direct`. Retire sessions only through `lanes reap`: dry run at milestones and before ending coordination, then `--apply`, which has the lane write its own handoff and needs a named recipient to accept it (`lanes handoff ID --accept --from RECIPIENT`). Silence is not completion. Never mark claims done, remove worktrees or edit pi-questions identities to retire a session. `lanes snooze` deliberate waits; continue retired work with `lanes resume ID` or `lanes start NEW-ID --from-handoff ID`. `lanes reap` treats a lane as busy only for real work (an owned pi-phase run, a build/test process or CPU use); `lanes close` records closed state even after the worktree is gone.

  Lanes report to their repo orchestrator, not to L0: they deliver the finished branch (branch, SHA, gate evidence, release-note bullets) and move on without a PR. A repository with one lane needs no orchestrator. The rest of this section applies to the train PR, not to individual changes.

- **Do not wait on events local tools remove.** Base work on the local candidate instead of waiting for it to merge; verify against locally built packages instead of waiting for a release (only the final pin bump waits); evict a stuck or failing item instead of holding the train (see "Evict, don't hold"). Only the build queue is a real wait, and it is used for independent work.
- **The train is the PR.** Before delivering, fold all finished branches/issues from the session (and closely related open work in the same repo) into the repository's open train: one release, one CI run, each issue its own release-note bullet with `(#<n>)`. Check `gh pr list --repo <owner/repo> --state open` first: a related open PR is the train to fold into (or fold and close), never a second PR. Unrelated, differently risky or differently labelled work rides a later train; it gets its own PR only under the exceptions above.
- **Fold superseded PRs immediately.** Once an existing PR's branch is (or will be) merged into the train, close the PR with "Folded into <train branch or #N>", cancel its queued or in-progress runs (`gh run list --branch <b> --json databaseId,status,headSha`, then `gh run cancel <id>`) and move its release-note bullets into the train's note. `pi-train fold` does this. Never push to a folded branch.
- **Cadence, not giant trains.** A train holds at most about 10 items, or one review slot of glue. Cut the next train the moment the previous one merges, or at least every 4 h while ready items wait; never hold the cut for a delivery that is not ready, and keep items blocked on a design, a consumer proof or another repository out. After the cut nothing joins. Record the cut decision in one line in the train's reviewer comment, not in the release note.
- **Evict, don't hold.** If train CI or the glue review fails because of one item, revert it (revert commit, no history rewrite), push once, ship the rest, and requeue the item after its fix. Diagnose an unattributable failure; never evict the whole train blindly. Merge as soon as CI is green and the review passed (minor/patch; majors stay with {{GITHUB_USER}}).
